Max Weber

German sociologist and economist (1864–1920); known for Verstehen, the ideal type, rationalization and the types of legitimate domination; in 1910 he proposed a survey on the sociology of the press.

Biography

Max Weber was born in Erfurt on 21 April 1864 and died in 1920. His father was a lawyer and politician. He trained in law and economics; he received his first university appointment at Freiburg in 1894 and two years later a professorship at Heidelberg. His health did not allow him to hold a permanent academic post for long.

According to the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy his two most celebrated contributions are the “rationalization thesis” and the “Protestant Ethic thesis”; The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism appeared in 1904–05. In methodology he defended interpreting action through its meaning (Verstehen) and constructing ideal types by one-sided accentuation of reality. In political sociology he distinguished three types of legitimate domination: traditional, charismatic and legal-rational. Economy and Society was published after his death.

Weber took part in founding the German Sociological Society together with Tönnies and Simmel. At its first congress in 1910 he proposed a “sociology of the press” as its first research topic; the project was abandoned in 1911.
