Charles Horton Cooley

American sociologist (1864–1929). With the “looking-glass self” he described how people build a sense of themselves through the judgments they imagine in other minds.

  • 1864–1929
  • Born: Ann Arbor, Michigan
  • sociologist

Biography

Charles Horton Cooley was born in Ann Arbor, Michigan, in 1864 and died in 1929. He taught at the University of Michigan throughout his career: according to an encyclopedia entry first political science and economics (1892–1904), then sociology (1904–1929). His main books are Human Nature and the Social Order (1902), Social Organization (1909) and Social Process (1918).

He is known for two ideas: the looking-glass self and the primary group. The looking-glass self says that a person builds a sense of self through the judgment they imagine others holding of them; in his 1902 book Cooley describes it with three elements: imagining how our appearance looks to the other person, imagining that person’s judgment of it, and a self-feeling such as pride or mortification. A primary group is one formed by close, face-to-face ties such as the family.

The encyclopedia entry calls his method “sympathetic introspection”: he tried to understand society less by measuring it from outside than by grasping, from within, the meanings people live by. He is separate from Mead and Blumer in Chicago but counted as a forerunner of the same tradition.
