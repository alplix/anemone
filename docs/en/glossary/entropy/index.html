Concept

entropy

Shannon’s measure of information: the number, in bits, that shows how much freedom of choice a source has among possible messages.

In Shannon’s theory, entropy measures “how much information a source produces”; but the information here is not the meaning of the message, it is freedom of choice. A choice between two equally likely options is 1 bit; 16 equally likely options are 4 bits (2 to the power 4 is 16). As one of the options becomes dominant, uncertainty, and with it entropy, falls; if a message is known for certain, entropy is zero.

The measure rests on the logarithm of probabilities; the idea of a logarithmic measure goes back to Hartley’s 1928 work. The ratio of a source’s actual entropy to the highest entropy it could reach with the same symbols is “relative entropy”; one minus it is “redundancy”. Shannon writes that the redundancy of English is roughly 50 per cent: half of the letters we write come from the structure of the language, and half we choose freely. Redundancy looks like waste but is insurance against noise: you can recover a corrupted letter from context.

In Turkish sources, alongside “entropi”, the terms “enformasyonda eksiklik” and “fazlalık” (redundancy) also occur (Yaylagül 2006). It has a mathematical resemblance to entropy in physics, but here it is a statistical measure. Details: The Lasswell and Shannon–Weaver Models.

Example

A number between 1 and 16 has been chosen and every number is equally likely: 4 yes/no questions (4 bits) are enough to find the right number.
