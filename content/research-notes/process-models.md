## (0) Corrections to the brief (read this first)

- **MacLean's dates in the brief (1913-2001) are wrong or at least unconfirmed.** I found only conflicting data (one source 1920-1974, another 1930-1972, and a Wikipedia-derived summary of a different person). No life dates are given for MacLean. For Westley, 1915-1990 appears on one reference page and on a genealogy snippet, but I could not open a second reliable source; his dates are omitted too. Both people pages therefore have no `born`/`died` and rest on the 1957 article's own editor's note (Westley at Wisconsin, MacLean at Michigan State, both teachers of journalism) plus, for MacLean, Berlo's 1960 preface (thanks to "Malcolm MacLean, Junior" as a colleague at Michigan State). I did not verify that this MacLean is the same person who later directed the Iowa journalism school; the lesson does not say so.
- **Berlo's own book does not use the acronym "SMCR", and its factor lists differ from the popular version.** In the OCR text (Digital Library of India scan, archive.org `dli.ernet.235577`) the model has six components (source, encoder, message, channel, decoder, receiver); "SMCR" was not found (OCR is imperfect, so the lesson says this with a caveat). Berlo lists four factors for the source-encoder (skills, attitudes, knowledge level, social-cultural system) and three for the message (code, content, treatment, each with elements and structure); popular web summaries list five and five. The lesson has a deep card on this.
- **Schramm's texts do not use the labels "Osgood-Schramm", "circular" or "field of experience".** I read the whole 1954 chapter (4th printing scan of the 1954 volume) and the 1971 rewrite (full-text PDF). 1954: the chain source-encoder-signal-decoder-destination; the two circles are "the accumulated experience" of the two people; each person is both an encoder and a decoder; the process is "really endless"; a footnote thanks Osgood for "the model in the following pages", i.e. the learning-theory diagram; feedback is "the return process". 1971: Schramm says he wrote the paper in 1952; the circles are "frames of reference ... fund of usable experience"; Osgood "has preferred to schematize the process" as encoder-decoder-interpreter with each participant receiving and sending (footnote 11: Osgood, *A Vocabulary for Talking about Communication*, Urbana, n.d.). So which parts are Osgood's: the learning-theory (mediation) diagram (1954 footnote) and the encoder/decoder/interpreter scheme (1971 credit). The overlapping circles and the endless-process remark are in Schramm's own text with no Osgood credit. The Wikipedia claim that Osgood "posited a field of shared experience" is not supported by Schramm's footnote and is not used.
- **Berlo did not say "bullet".** Already established in the `magic-bullet` lesson; here it is used only as "hypodermic needle" (Berlo 1960, pp. 27-28) against a process view.
- **"The transmission model was a straw man":** I found no scholarly source that applies the words "straw man" to the transmission (Shannon-Weaver/Lasswell) model as such. The words are used for the hypodermic-needle story (Pooley's typology; Lubken 2008 title). The deep card therefore separates three positions and says so: (1) Sapienza et al. 2016 on the field's dominant stage narrative; (2) the same paper on the origins of the transmission metaphor (not Shannon-Weaver's invention; Shannon literal, Weaver metaphorical); (3) Pooley's "strawman predecessor" use of disciplinary history (Katz and Lazarsfeld 1955 example). The card states that Sapienza et al. do not use the label.
- **Dance's helix** (brief: "only if verified"): left out. I found the book (Dance, ed., *Human Communication Theory*, Holt, Rinehart and Winston, 1967; Open Library, Social Forces review) but only low-quality web summaries of the helix itself and never opened the chapter.
