# Research notes: lesson `mass-media-history` (2026-09-20)

Author: Claude (batch: mass-media-history lesson; people gutenberg, muteferrika, day; concepts mass-media, movable-type, penny-press, news-agency, broadcasting; bibliography `content/bibliography/mass-media-history.json`, 60+ entries, all `verified.status: ok`).

Method: every date and fact in the lesson was checked against at least two sources I opened (web page, Crossref/OpenAlex record with abstract, Open Library, or a full text). Several large reference sites (Britannica, Senate.gov, British Library, Library of Congress item pages, Project MUSE, H-Net, Princeton DPUL, the MEB module PDF) refused automated access (bot check, 403, TLS error) and were **not** used. I did not bypass those blocks. Where an article was only read at abstract level this is said in the bibliography `note` and only abstract-level claims are made.

## (a) Could not verify, therefore left out

Printing
- **Gutenberg's birth year** (estimates 1393-1406 in one source; "about 1400" in others): the person file has only the death year 1468 (Library of Congress guide + National Diet Library page agree).
- **Outcome of the 1455 lawsuit / whether Fust "ruined" Gutenberg.** The LoC guide says the evidence does not support the legend; the NDL page says he lost his equipment. Left out entirely.
- **The Catholicon (1460)**: LoC says confidently attributed to Gutenberg, NDL says believed not printed by him. Left out.
- **Number of surviving Bibles (49 in one source)**, Frankfurt fair of October 1454 (only in search snippets), the Mainz Psalter of 1457 as "first book with a printed date and colophon" (search snippets and a Princeton page that only returned a bot check): left out.
- **Which press design Gutenberg used (wine press?)** and the "Work of the Books" phrase: not verified, left out.
- **Luther and the pamphlet explosion** (numbers of pamphlets): no verified source opened; the lesson uses only Rubin 2014 for the Reformation link.
- **The 1487/1488 "Leçons des Enfants" question** (first book printed in Istanbul?): a Penn Libraries guide lists a study with that title; not opened. The lesson therefore does not say the 1493 Hebrew Arba'ah Turim was "the first book printed in Istanbul"; it says printing was introduced there in 1493 by exiles from Spain and Portugal (Encyclopaedia Judaica text on Jewish Virtual Library; Schwartz 2017), and names the book from the title of Offenberg's chapter (Crossref record only; chapter not read).

Newspapers and agencies
- **Circulation numbers of the Sun** (8,000 / 19,360 / 26,000 / 30,000 depending on source and date) and the claim that the Moon Hoax "trebled" circulation: sources conflict (Museum of Hoaxes vs. others). None used.
- **Whether the Sun ever admitted the hoax** (History.com says 16 September 1835; Linda Hall and the Museum of Hoaxes say never publicly). The lesson follows the two that agree.
- **Wolff's Berlin agency (1849)**: only in search snippets; year left out (Wolff is named only as one of the three agencies).
- **AP**: May 1846 pooling of five New York papers for Mexican War news rests on New World Encyclopedia (Wikipedia-derived), a second summary (Daily Cartoonist) and Teaching American History (pony express and telegraph). AP's own history page was blocked. Modest sourcing; the lesson gives only year, purpose and the war. The date "22 May 1846" is NOT used.
- **Agencies' 1869/1870 territorial agreements**: Encyclopedia.com gives 1869 for Europe; other summaries give 1870 for the world. The lesson says "late 1860s" and Europe only.
- **Death year of Reuter**: one page's "1878" is his retirement; 1899 is confirmed by Encyclopedia.com and Wikipedia. I dropped a Reuter person file (not needed).
- **Inverted pyramid**: only Pöttker 2003's abstract was read; full text and the counter-literature (e.g., Schudson-type arguments) were not.

Visual media and broadcasting
- **Lumière audience size** (33 vs about 40): left out. Price of admission: not found.
- **KDKA broadcast hours** (6 p.m. to noon in one ETHW page, 8 p.m. to midnight elsewhere): left out. "First commercial station" is stated as "one of the first commercial radio stations".
- **BBC TV "first"**: EBSCO and Alexandra Palace both say "first regular high-definition"; EBSCO adds that Germany began a regular 180-line service a year earlier. Included with the definition made explicit.
- **Halftone**: the LoC catalogue page (Daily Graphic 4 March 1880, p. 38) came only from a search snippet; the fetch was blocked. The date rests on Wikipedia, Encyclopedia.com and HistoryofInformation.
- **The 1873 "Steinway Hall" earlier photograph in the Daily Graphic** (granulated, not halftone): only in one Wikipedia page; left out.
- **ARPANET (October 1969)**: one source only (Internet Society); left out. No social-media platform launch dates used (none verified twice).

Turkey
- **Müteferrika's birth (1670-1674 in TDV; 1674 or 1675 elsewhere) and death (1745 or 1747)**: sources disagree; the person file has no dates and the text says "mid-1740s".
- **Exact number of Müteferrika's works**: TDV Müteferrika Matbaası says 16-17 works in 22 volumes, TDV İbrahim Müteferrika says 17 books in 23 volumes, English Wikipedia says 17 titles in 23 volumes. The lesson says "about seventeen (16-17 by source)".
- **Copies printed per title** (500-1,200) and the "70% sold" claim in a Daily Sabah feature: not used.
- **First issue day of Takvim-i Vekayi**: TDV says 25 Cemaziyelevvel 1247 (given as 1 November 1831) and 5 November 1831 for Le Moniteur ottoman; several other Turkish pages say 11 November 1831. The lesson says only "November 1831".
- **Ankara Radio's start**: TRT's own page says 1928; KÜRE/AA say November 1927; TRT says "officially opened 28 October 1938". Only 1938 is used. Date of the company Türk Telsiz Telefon A.Ş. (8 September 1927 in KÜRE) not used.
- **Anadolu Ajansı**: AA archive says "five days before Parliament", another says 17 days; not used. First news bulletin on 12 April 1920 (KÜRE only): not used.
- **1993 constitutional amendment freeing private broadcasting** (KÜRE only): not used.
- **İTÜ's 1952 television experiment** (search summary from a TRT news item): left out (the lesson names no university anyway).
- **Ceride-i Havadis**: date given as 31 July / 1 August 1840 and "state support" vs "state permission": only the year and "private, with state permission" are used (Journo).
- **Tercümân-ı Ahvâl as "first private Turkish-language newspaper"**: TDV wording; Ceride-i Havadis was also private (English owner). The lesson phrases it "issued by Turks without state support".
- **Armenian press (1567), Greek press (1627), Murad III's 1587 decree** appear only in a Daily Sabah feature: left out. Schwartz confirms a Murad III firman but I did not extract its year.
- **Jewish press in Thessaloniki (1493)** (Daily Sabah): left out.

## (b) Uncertainties that remain in the text

- The "four questions" framework (production, reach, speed, control) is my own teaching device and is labelled as such on the card; the digital "four answers" on the `today-digital` card are labelled as the lesson's interpretation.
- Statements about Gutenberg's role ("early and documented pioneer") are cautious on purpose because the attribution of the 42-line Bible is by general acceptance (Keio catalogue) and the 31-line indulgence attribution is disputed (Wikipedia).
- `turkey-press` last paragraph and `today-digital` are interpretation, labelled "this is the lesson's interpretation".
- The Turkish gloss "penny press (bir kuruşluk gazete)" is my own descriptive gloss; no established Turkish term was verified. Terminology status `check`.
- The plain-language remark that Turkish "yayın" carries the verb "yaymak" is my own observation (not from a source).
- Terminology statuses marked `check` (kolofon, yarı ton, ters piramit, hareketli harf) need a Turkish-language reviewer.
