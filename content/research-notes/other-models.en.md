## Adaptations
- **Spelling**: British (organisation, programme, colour), matching the other English course files.
- **Titles and slugs** follow the English `course.json`: "Other Models: Jakobson, Gerbner, Maletzke", slug `other-models`. Concept slugs are the translated terms; person slugs are the names.
- **Turkish terms kept on purpose** in the `deep-names` and `exam-patterns` cards (and in concept `altTerms` only where they are the point): those cards are about how Turkish sources name things (göndergesel, duygusal, çağrı, kanal teyidi, irtibat teyidi, metadil, poetik, üstdil, ilişki işlevi, iletişim şeması, Gerbner Modeli, Maletzke modeli). Each is explained in English in the same sentence.
- **Turkish example sentences** were rendered so that they teach the same thing: "Uçak kalkmıştır" → "The plane has left", "Eyvah, uçak kalkmıştır" → "Oh no, the plane has left", "Maskesiz girilmez" → "No entry without a mask", "Alo, sesim geliyor mu?" → "Hello, can you hear me?". The *Mesnevi* opening "Dinle, bu ney neler anlatır" is given in the English rendering "Listen to this reed, what it tells" (our translation of the Turkish line in Nesterova Coşkun's article; not a published English translation).
- **"I like Ike"** stays in English in both versions; the Turkish lesson explains the sound analysis in Turkish, the English lesson gives it directly.
- **"sen" voice**: Turkish direct address became "you"; the dry humour was kept where it teaches (e.g. "code" for the letters of Gerbner's model).
- **Quiz option wording**: `q3` (match) and `q4` (order) translated one-to-one; answer indexes and item order are identical; `q6` case parts keep ids q6a-c.
- **Concept names**: "kanal teyidi (fatik) işlevi" → "phatic (channel-checking) function"; "üstdilsel" → "metalingual"; "araçtan gelen zorlama" → "constraint of the medium" (Maletzke's *Zwang des Mediums*); "parafeedback" → "para-feedback".
- **Descriptions** trimmed to 160 characters; people `short` fields under 200 characters.
- Quotations: the lesson contains no quotations from sources in either language; the German terms and the English/German titles are kept in the original.

## Doubts
- "field schema" for *Feldschema*: the English literature also says "field model" and calls it "Maletzke's model of the mass communication process" (McQuail & Windahl); I kept "field schema" to match the German and the Turkish literal rendering.
- "Communicator" (McQuail & Windahl, Maletzke) is kept for *Kommunikator*; "recipient" for *Rezipient*, "receiver" only inside Jakobson/Gerbner contexts as the sources do.
- No sentence was dropped or added relative to the Turkish source; the two edits made to the Turkish after review (softening of the Burkart sentence, the Semioticon attribution) are included in both languages.
