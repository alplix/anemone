## (a) Could NOT verify, therefore left out (or used only with attribution)

**Primary works not opened**
- **Lazarsfeld 1941** (the essay itself, JSTOR/PDC): not read. The lesson uses only what McQuail 2010 (critical vs administrative orientation), Shepperd 2022 (a short quotation, “tool handled by people or agencies for given purposes”) and Pooley & Jeřábek 2022 (the label was Adorno's shorthand; the essay appeared in the Frankfurt Institute's journal) report about it. A search-engine summary attributed to the essay more specific wording (administrative research asks about audience size and composition; critical research asks “How are these media organized and controlled?”); I could not check it against the text and did not use it.
- **Craig 1999** full text: only the abstract (Crossref, Oxford Academic, OpenAlex) was read. The one-line “communication theorized as …” phrases come from a LibreTexts table that cites Craig 1999 (Table 4.1); four of the seven phrases were seen again in a second teaching text in search results (not opened, 403). The lesson therefore gives the phrases in our own Turkish wording and does not quote them as Craig's words beyond short fragments.
- **Littlejohn & Foss**, *Theories of Human Communication*: the book was not opened. The six evaluation criteria (scope, parsimony, heuristic value, openness, appropriateness, validity) are attributed to them via Wikibooks (which names them) and a LOUIS/LibreTexts open textbook (same list, no attribution). The lesson says “textbooks summarise Littlejohn and Foss's list as …”.
- **Popper**: *Logik der Forschung* / *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* not read; everything comes from the Stanford Encyclopedia (Thornton) and the IEP (Shea; author name as reported by the page fetch). Conjectures and Refutations (1963, the source of the Einstein/Freud/Adler comparison in Popper's own words) not opened.
- **Kuhn**: book not read; SEP (Bird) and the MIT obituary used. The number of senses of “paradigm” (Masterman) is deliberately not given.
- **Meyrowitz 2008**, **Potter, Cooper & Dupagne 1993**: abstracts only (OpenAlex/Oxford Academic). The percentages (over 60 / about 34 / under 6) are from the abstract of Potter et al.; the coding rules and the eight journals' names are not known to me.
- **Fink & Gantz 1996, Gitlin 1978, Golding & Murdock 1978, Windahl & Signitzer 2007, Tunstall 1977**: cited only as reported by McQuail 2010 or by Pooley & Jeřábek; the original works were not opened. Gitlin 1978 is used only through Pooley & Jeřábek's description.
- **Berger & Chaffee 1987** definition of communication science: used only as quoted in McQuail 2010, without the quotation itself.
- **Bryant & Miron 2004** (“Theory and research in mass communication”, Journal of Communication 54(4)): the abstract is not available in Crossref/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar and the Oxford page was paywalled. I would have liked to use it for “which theories the field actually uses”; **left out**.
- **Lewin's own text** (1943, *Journal of Social Psychology* 17(1), 113–131; Crossref-verified): the deep card does not say what Lewin wrote. It says the saying is “usually attributed to Lewin” (McCain 2015: 693 articles; Bedeian 2016: traced to General Electric in the 1920s and to Dörpfeld 1873). A Wikiquote page quoting Weick 2003 says Lewin attributed the line to “a business man”; not used (secondary quotation of a secondary source).
- **Turkish articles** (Erdoğan, Keloğlu-İşler & Durmuş 2005; Yavuz 2015): only titles, abstracts and keywords were read. The article by Erdoğan et al. is used for its title and topic (Lazarsfeld's “yönetimsel araştırmalar” and the positivist empirical tradition). The author order differs between a university repository record (Durmuş Şenyapar, Erdoğan, Keloğlu İşler) and the journal's own 2005/21 table of contents (Erdoğan, Keloğlu-İşler, Durmuş); I used the journal's order.
- **Turkish translations** of Popper and Kuhn: the Kuhn title (*Bilimsel Devrimlerin Yapısı*, tr. Nilüfer Kuyaş, Kırmızı Yayınları) was confirmed on the publisher page; the Popper title (*Bilimsel Araştırmanın Mantığı*, Yapı Kredi Yayınları, tr. İlknur Aka and İbrahim Turan) only in search-result listings (the publisher page returned 403). **Neither is mentioned in the lesson.**
- **Turkish equivalents of terms**: only “orta boy kuramlar” (a Turkish sociology page by Ömer Yıldırım, citing a Turkish open-education textbook and Ritzer 2008) and “yönetimsel araştırma”, “egemen ve eleştirel yaklaşımlar”, “anadamar/çoğulcu”, “yöndeşme” (titles/keywords of the three Turkish sources above) were seen in sources I opened. “Orta erimli/orta menzilli kuram” and “idari araştırma” appeared only in search-engine summaries and are **not** used as attested names (the lesson uses “idari (yönetimsel)” as a rendering, see terminology file: status ok for the pair, ‘idari’ itself unverified).

**Numbers and details deliberately omitted**
- The Lazarsfeld 1949 list also has three items about race (Black vs. white soldiers' ambition, Southern Black soldiers' preferences). They are part of the same list but rely on stereotypes and are not needed for the point; omitted. The rural/city item is used in the lesson, but the reveal names only the education and climate items (Lazarsfeld says every item is “the direct opposite”; for the rural item the opposite is not spelled out in the text).
- Merton's 1949 date: the excerpt read is an anthology reprint headed “[1949]” whose publisher line (Simon & Schuster, The Free Press) is that of a later edition; wording may derive from an enlarged edition. Attributed to “Merton (1949)” as in the reprint.
- Popper's *Logik der Forschung* date: SEP, IEP and Open Library say 1935 (Wien, J. Springer); some sources say 1934. The lesson says 1935.
- Merton's exact page range for the chapter, Boyle/Pascal history (used only as Merton reports it: “Boyle … sea of air … as Pascal inferred”).
- McQuail's “five” vs “four” kinds: the fourth edition (2000) was read only as quoted in a course handout (Siregar), the sixth (2010) directly. The fifth edition (2005) was not checked, so the lesson does not say when “cultural theory” was added, only that the 2000 edition lists four and the 2010 edition five. (One search result suggests the fifth edition may already list five; unverified.)
- Craig's date of birth and other private details: omitted (living person).
- Potter–Cooper–Dupagne's first author **W. James Potter** was not linked to the existing `potter` person file (cultivation critic). The name is the same, and it is very likely the same scholar, but I did not confirm it.

## (b) Uncertainties and judgment calls

- **Copy of McQuail 2010**: read through a third-party-hosted PDF found by search; used only for fact-checking (all wording is ours). Page numbers not cited.
- **“Merceğin” (lens) metaphor**, the “five-question compass”, and the mapping of Meyrowitz's three camps to “power / pleasure / pattern” are ours; labelled “bizim yorumumuz”/“our interpretation” where they might be mistaken for sources. The mapping follows the order of McQuail's report and the abstract but is an inference.
- **Applying middle-range to agenda-setting, cultivation, spiral of silence** is our reading (labelled). Merton did not write about media effects in that chapter.
- **Hypothetical scenarios** (all labelled “Hayal et / Imagine”): the “resistance effect” theory, the editor who shortens headlines, the question-mark headline student, the newsroom rule, the two mini-theories A and B, the youth-platform misinformation lab.
- **Reused documented facts** from the earlier lessons (each verified there): 1968 violence commission (Shanahan & Morgan 1999), Chapel Hill study of undecided voters (McCombs & Shaw 1972), 2023 social-media cultivation meta-analysis (Hermann, Morgan & Shanahan 2023: 66 samples, 460 effect sizes, .123), Pew 2013 (Hampton et al. 2014: 86% / 42%).
- **Shepperd 2022** presents one scholar's archival reading of the Princeton Radio Research Project; Pooley & Jeřábek 2022 (an editorial introduction) present a revisionist view of Lazarsfeld. The deep card says the history is contested.
- **`minutes` in course.json (30)**: with 25 core cards the lesson will probably take closer to 35–40 minutes; left for the lead to decide.
- **Card length**: a few core cards (evidence-journals, how-classify, how-popper, turkey-terms) are 127–145 words by whitespace count, slightly under the 150-word guide; I preferred short and verified to longer and uncertain.
