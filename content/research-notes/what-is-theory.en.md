## Adaptations
- **Spelling**: British (organisation, colour, behaviour, generalisation), matching the existing English files.
- **Turkish terms kept inside the English text, with a gloss**, only where the Turkish literature itself is the topic: the `turkey-terms` and `exam-patterns` cards keep “yönetimsel araştırma”, “yöndeşme”, “anadamar”, “çoğulcu”, “egemen”, “eleştirel/radikal”, “orta boy kuram” and give the English meaning next to them. These cards are the only place where Turkish letters remain in the English lesson file (as do the Czech/German/Turkish names Jeřábek, Dörpfeld, Erdoğan, Keloğlu-İşler, Durmuş, Yaylagül, İletişim). Concept files list no Turkish alt terms in English.
- **`turkey-terms`**: the section is about Turkish academic writing; the English adds the glosses “dominant”, “mainstream”, “pluralist”, “administrative”, “convergence” so a foreign reader can follow.
- **Craig's “communication theorized as …” phrases**: the Turkish card gives them in Turkish (our wording, marked as such); the English restores the English phrases (“practical art of discourse”, “intersubjective mediation by signs”, “experience of otherness”, “information processing”, “expression, interaction and influence”, “(re)production of social order”, “discursive reflection”) as they appear in the LibreTexts table that cites Craig 1999. Each is at most 8 words.
- **“Mercek/lens”**: “Mercek” (lens) translated literally; the Turkish “bizim yorumumuz” became “Our interpretation”.
- **“Egemen paradigma”** → “dominant paradigm”; **“alternatif (eleştirel) paradigma”** → “alternative (critical) paradigm”; **“orta düzey kuram”** → “middle-range theory”; **“medya-merkezli / toplum-merkezli”** → “media-centric / society-centric”; **“kültürcü / maddeci”** → “culturalist / materialist”; **“sosyo-psikolojik / sosyo-kültürel gelenek”** → “sociopsychological / sociocultural tradition”.
- **Exam card**: the Turkish alternative names (e.g. “orta boy kuram” for middle-range theory) are kept in Turkish inside the English `exam-patterns` card because the card is about how the topic is named in Turkish sources; the closing sentence about general exam-question patterns was translated.
- **Person biographies**: the Turkish `inCourse` sentences pointing to “Orta Düzey Etkiler” were translated with the English unit title “Mid-Range Effects”.
- **Slugs**: lesson slug `what-is-a-theory` and person/concept slugs follow the English names; they match `course.json` (lesson) and are unique per language.
- **Question texts** were translated one-to-one; answer indexes, option counts, pairs, items and blanks are identical (validator parity: 0 errors).

## Doubts
- “Sosyal bilimsel” → “social scientific” follows McQuail's own English term (“social scientific theory”), not “social-science”.
- “Yorumlayıcı” → “interpretive” (McQuail 2010 uses “interpretative” in one place and “interpretive” in Potter et al.; the lesson uses the shorter form).
- Quotation marks: the English keeps the Turkish curly quotation style; no quotation over 15 words in either language.
