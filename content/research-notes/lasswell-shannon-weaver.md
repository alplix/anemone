## (a) Could not verify, therefore left out (or explicitly hedged)

**Lasswell 1948 (the central primary source)**
- **The essay text itself was never opened.** The only copies are lending-library scans (Internet Archive: `communicationofi0000lyma`, `…_l4x8`, `…_z6p5`, `communicationofi00jewi`: full text returns HTTP 401/empty; HathiTrust catalogue 403; Google Books API quota exceeded; Scribd, Academia, studylib, ResearchGate blocked or only AI summaries). Consequences:
  - The lesson does **not quote** the essay. The five questions are given in Turkish/English paraphrase, in italics or bold, without quotation marks. The exact English capitalisation and the wording "In Which Channel" versus "In What Channel" differ between secondary sources (Wikipedia: "In What Channel"; communicationtheory.org and Encyclopedia.com: "in which channel"), so the lesson does not pretend to reproduce the original casing.
  - The three functions come from two secondary summaries that agree (Yaylagül 2006 in Turkish; the iResearchNet "Functionalism" entry, which paraphrases Lasswell and cites the Schramm 1960 reprint, p. 118). They are stated as Lasswell's list, in my words.
  - "Control / content / media / audience / effect analysis": Wikipedia (English) plus Yaylagül 2006 (content, media, audience, effect; the word before "analizi" for the first item is garbled in the scan) plus Turkish web summaries for "kontrol analizi". Flagged `check` in the terminology file.
  - Pages 37-51 (Bryson volume) come from Sapienza et al. 2015 and Wikipedia; Yaylagül cites the start page 37. The Schramm 1960 reprint pages (117-130 / 117-129) differ between sources; not used.
  - "Lasswell was less interested in dividing up the act of communication than in seeing it as a whole" is the iResearchNet paraphrase of p. 118 of the reprint; used as reported, cited, not as a quotation.
  - Whether the essay itself draws a diagram: **not verified**. The lesson only says that textbooks present the list as boxes and arrows and that some scholars prefer "formula" or "construct". (A Wikipedia summary claims Lasswell "never devised" a diagram; I could not confirm it and did not use it.)
  - Which lecture in the Nov 1946 - Feb 1947 series was Lasswell's: unknown (catalogue note says only that the volume is "based principally on" those lectures).
- **First known reference to the model in 1940**: taken from the *abstract* of Sapienza, Iyer & Veenstra 2015 (paywalled). I do not know what the 1940 reference is. Used only in the deep card `deep-dates` with "according to the abstract" phrasing.
- **Lasswell's list of who performs each function** (diplomats/attachés/correspondents; editors/journalists; educators): seen only inside a web-search summary of the essay, never on a page I opened. Left out.
- **Entertainment as fourth function**: the claim "added by Wright (1960, p. 610)" rests on the iResearchNet entry (secondary); Wright's article itself was not read (Crossref/OpenAlex only). Yaylagül 2006 independently says entertainment and market roles "can be added" and lists only three Lasswell functions. Several web pages I saw (a student term paper, a Wikipedia article on uses and gratifications, teaching sites) wrongly credit four functions to Lasswell; they are not cited. The lesson says the addition is "generally attributed to Wright".
- **Who coined "hypodermic needle" / "magic bullet" and when**: not verified, deliberately not claimed. Sproule 1989 and Bineham 1988 were used from Crossref + OpenAlex abstracts only (full texts paywalled). Lubken 2008, Chaffee & Hochheimer 1985, Katz 1987 etc. not opened. The word "hypodermic" does not occur in the full text of *Propaganda Technique in the World War* (searched in the OCR text of the 1938 reprint), but the book does use an injection image once (Wilson's rhetoric as a "subtle poison" that "industrious men injected into the veins"); the lesson says so in `deep-needle`.
- **Lasswell biography**: Britannica and the Yale Archives finding aid returned 403 / a JavaScript challenge and were not opened; they are not cited. Sources disagree on the Library of Congress war communications post (1939-1945 vs 1939-1946) and on his Chicago years (1922-1938 vs "joined the faculty in 1926"), so neither appears. "Professor of law" rests on Yaylagül 2006 only; the bio therefore says "hukuk ve siyaset bilimi dersleri" (taught law and political science) via Wikipedia's Yale 1946-1970 plus Yaylagül. Doctorate year (1926) is not stated (Encyclopedia.com people entry and Wikipedia give different framings).
- **Universities**: the lesson text names no university (project rule). Bios say "a university on the US east coast" and "Chicago" only as a place.

**Shannon, Weaver, Bell Labs**
- **Departure from Bell Labs**: MacTutor says he "remained at the Bell Laboratories until 1972", MIT News says "affiliated with Bell Laboratories from 1941 to 1972", Horgan (IEEE Spectrum, 1992/2016) says "went to Bell Laboratories in 1941 and remained there for 15 years". The lesson states 1941 and "about fifteen years" (Horgan) and never "1972".
- **Weaver's Rockefeller Foundation dates**: IEEE Computer Society and AAAS agree on 1932-1955 as director of Natural Sciences; MacTutor's later posts differ slightly. Only 1932-1955 is used. Weaver's essay was read in a course-material copy dated September 1949 (UC Berkeley I School), not in the printed book; the printed book was not opened. That Weaver was "not a Bell Labs employee" is inferred from the IEEE-CS career listing (no Bell Labs post) and from Weaver's own abstract, which names Shannon as "of the Bell Telephone Laboratories" separately.
- **Fire-control "noise" story** (`deep-bell`): single source, Guizzo 2003 (thesis). Indirect support only: Bode & Shannon 1950 (Crossref + abstract) co-authored on smoothing and prediction. The Turing-Shannon lunches (Jan 1943), the "X System" and Turing's "ban" appear in Guizzo only and were left out of the lesson.
- **Transistor announcement (1948) and "golden years of Bell Labs"**: only search-summary level; left out.
- **Nyquist 1924/1928**: named only through Shannon's footnote 1; the papers were not opened (no bib entry).
- **Hartley 1928**: the OCR text on the Internet Archive was read (synopsis and section "Elimination of Psychological Factors"). Hartley's formula H = n log s is not stated in the lesson.
- **Memorandum number of the 1945 cryptography report**: the scan's cover sheet OCR reads "45-110-92" while web sources say "45-110-02"; the number is omitted from the bib entry.
- **Shannon quote about meaning** ("can't and wasn't intended to address"): comes from Horgan's profile (a journalist's report of Shannon's remark); the lesson paraphrases it, says "in one profile", and does not date the remark.
- **QR code levels**: the L/M/Q/H percentages (7/15/25/30) were read from Denso Wave's own table (an image on their page). The claim that Denso Wave "developed" QR codes was not checked on a page I opened, so the lesson says only "Denso Wave's page". A one-line statement on that page that Reed-Solomon codes were originally developed for satellite and probe communication was not used.
- **Turbo codes**: only the Crossref/OpenAlex abstract (performance "close to the Shannon limit"); no numbers given. Year 1993 taken from the proceedings title "ICC '93".
- **Zimmermann telegram (lab)**: dates differ between sources (LoC guide says the telegram was sent January 19, other accounts January 16; decoding dates vary). The lesson uses only what NARA, the LoC guide and the National WWI Museum agree on: early 1917; British deciphering; 24 Feb presentation to the US government; publication on 1 March; declaration of war on 6 April; the 1914 cable-cutting and the tapping of borrowed neutral lines. Zimmermann's admission is dated 3 March (LoC) or 29 March (Museum: Reichstag speech); the lesson gives neither. Detail about line corruption/noise in the actual transmission: none in the sources; the lab says so instead of inventing an example.
- **Carey 1989**: the book was not read. The lesson only states that he contrasts a "transmission view" with a "ritual view", as summarised by the Wikipedia article on the ritual view (which names Lasswell's formula as the classic transmission-view statement) and the Open Library / Wikipedia records of the book. A first-hand check should come with the `ritual-view` lesson.
- **McQuail & Windahl 1993** ("formula until boxes and arrows are drawn") and **Braddock 1958**'s two added questions are reported through Wikipedia (English, and Turkish for Braddock); neither source was opened. Braddock's article exists (Crossref).
- **Bar-Hillel & Carnap**: Crossref record for the 1953 BJPS paper; the SEP entry (Sequoiah-Grayson & Floridi) says their (1952 outline of a) theory of semantic information was the first detailed one and that its language is monadic predicate logic. Neither paper was read.
- **Wright 1959 book** (*Mass Communication: A Sociological Perspective*) is mentioned by web summaries; not used.
- **"Entropy = thermodynamic entropy"**: Weaver's essay draws the analogy (and quotes Eddington); the lesson says only "mathematical resemblance", not that the two are the same.

## (b) Judgement calls

- Section `turkey` **omitted**. I looked for a documented Turkish case (Ottoman telegraph 1855) but the sources I could see give different dates and I could not find two solid ones; the Turkish literature is covered through the `exam` card (alternative names) and two Turkish textbooks used as evidence of how the models are taught.
- The Shannon-Weaver *model* is presented as it appears in Shannon's Fig. 1 (source, transmitter, [signal], receiver, destination, noise source). Shannon's paper lists five parts (source, transmitter, channel, receiver, destination); the noise source is drawn in the figure. The lesson says exactly that.
- **Turkish main terms**: "Lasswell formülü", "Shannon–Weaver modeli", "gürültü", "verici", "alıcı", "kanal", "hedef", "entropi", "fazlalık"; "ilişkilendirme" for correlation and "kontrol analizi" flagged `check` in `content/terminology/lasswell-shannon-weaver.json`. Yaylagül 2006 spells "Shanon" and "Laswel" (OCR of the scan is also noisy); İplikçi 2015 writes "Laswell". The lesson uses the correct spellings and lists the variants in `exam-patterns`.
- `deep-dates`: Turkish sources date the Shannon-Weaver model 1949 (Yaylagül) or 1948 (Turkish Wikipedia); the lesson explains why both occur instead of picking one.
- `crit-needle` is a fair-minded myth card: Lasswell 1927 does call propaganda one of the most powerful instruments of the modern world; the same chapter warns that its precise effect is largely surmise and that defeated countries exaggerate it. The 1927 passages ("one of the most powerful instrumentalities in the modern world", "largely a matter of surmise", the sentence about a "subtle poison" that men "injected into the veins" of a people, the definition of propaganda) are **paraphrased**; only the two words "subtle poison" appear in quotation marks, exactly, in the English `deep-needle` card. No quotation over 15 words appears anywhere; exact quotations used: "These semantic aspects of communication are irrelevant to the engineering problem" (11 words, Shannon 1948), "something of a scientific bandwagon" and "not a trivial matter of translating words to a new domain" (Shannon 1956), the ten-word sample "attack on an English writer that the character of this" (Shannon 1948), and the nonsense string "XFOML RXKHRJFFJUJ".
- The lab card's mapping of Lasswell's chapter structure (organisation / themes / results) onto who / what / effect is marked as **my reading**; Lasswell does not present it that way.
- `today-digital`: the reasoning about viral posts and algorithmic channels is explicitly labelled as ours, not documented research; the "language models predict the next piece" remark in `deep-entropy` is likewise labelled ours.
- Stories: every "Hayal et" (Imagine) scene is labelled hypothetical; the documented cases are the 1948 double publication, Hartley's ball-in-three-pockets example, Shannon's 1956 editorial and the Zimmermann telegram.
- Deep cards (6): `deep-bell`, `deep-dates`, `deep-functions`, `deep-entropy`, `deep-semantic`, `deep-needle`.
