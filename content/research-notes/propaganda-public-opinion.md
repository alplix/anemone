## (0) Corrections to the brief (read this first)

- **“Manufacture of consent” earlier in Bernays’s hands: not found.** Lippmann’s phrase is from *Public Opinion* (1922), chapter XV, section 4 (Gutenberg text, ebook 6456; it occurs once; the same paragraph also has “creation of consent” and “create consent”). Bernays’s phrase is “engineering of consent”, the title of his article in *The Annals* (250(1), March 1947, 113–120; Crossref-checked). The two online transcriptions I could read (bsahely.com transcript; Wikipedia summary of the article) contain neither “manufacture of consent” nor Lippmann’s name. I did **not** read Bernays’s *Crystallizing Public Opinion* (1923) or *Propaganda* (1928) line by line, so the lesson says only that I could not confirm an earlier Bernays use and does not claim he never wrote it. Chomsky (talk of 15 March 1989, chomsky.info) says Lippmann “invented the phrase”. The card `deep-consent-phrase` states exactly this.
- **“Propagandist as technician” is not Lasswell’s term.** The lesson says so (concept `propagandist-technician`, card `deep-technician`, “bu dersin kısaltması”). What is verified in his 1927 text (two OCR scans, 1927 London/New York edition and 1938 Peter Smith reprint): the book title *Propaganda Technique*; “Propaganda has become a profession”, “a corps of men who do nothing but study the ways and means of changing minds or binding minds to their convictions” (personnel section of the chapter on propaganda organization; the OCR running head reads “84” but the Contents put chapter II at pp. 14–46, so the page is probably 34: no page number is used); the quoted description of the “expert propagandist” by R. J. R. S. Wreford; “the working propagandist”; the definition (“the control of opinion by significant symbols … the management of opinions and attitudes by the direct manipulation of social suggestion rather than by altering other conditions in the environment or in the organism”, p. 9); propaganda as one of “three chief implements”; four strategic aims; the streetcar passage; “the task of the propagandist is usually to facilitate, rather than to fabricate”; the closing pages (“prevailing dogmas of sovereignty, of democracy…”, Anatole France’s “unseen engineer”).
- **“Lippmann’s own definition and its later fame”**: Lippmann’s own definition of public opinion (chapter I: the pictures inside heads are “their public opinions”; those acted upon by groups are “Public Opinion with capital letters”) is verified and used. The “later fame” of that definition as such could not be documented; the lesson documents instead the later fate of the *phrase* “manufacture of consent” and of the terms “pseudo-environment” and “stereotype”.
- **McCombs & Shaw 1972 and Lippmann**: the scanned text of the article (POQ 36, the copy used by the agenda-setting lesson) does not contain Lippmann’s name; it uses the term “pseudo-environment” (Table 5 discussion). The lesson says exactly that (card `deep-citations`). The agenda-setting lesson and the existing Lippmann person file say “McCombs and Shaw wrote that the pseudo-environment…”, consistent with this; the agenda-setting lesson’s statement that Iyengar, Peters & Kinder present their experiments as confirming Lippmann’s suspicion is confirmed by the abstract (OpenAlex/Crossref).
