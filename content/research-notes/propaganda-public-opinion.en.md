## Adaptations
- **Spelling**: British (colour, programme, criticise, favourable), matching the existing English files; “per cent” written out.
- **Quotations stay in the original English** wherever the Turkish text already quoted the English original (all at most 15 words; the 16-word sentence “every one of us is an outsider to all but a few aspects of modern life” is cut in both languages to “every one of us is an outsider”). Turkish glosses next to those English quotations (for example “yani uğursuz bir çınlama” after “an ominous clang”) were dropped or turned into plain English.
- **Turkish terms kept in Turkish with an English gloss** in: card `deep-turkish-terms` (kamuoyu, kanaat, rıza, propagandacı, hayalet kamu, sözde/sahte çevre, stereotip), card `exam-patterns` (alternative names in Turkish sources), the book title *Rızanın İmalatı*, the phrase “Kamuoyu” (the Turkish title of *Public Opinion*) in `deep-citations` and `claim-lippmann`. They are the Turkish names a Turkish reader meets and are exam-relevant; the same names are `altTerms` of the concepts.
- **`deep-turkish-terms`** is the one card written for Turkish readers; in English it explains the Turkish problem (the collective singular *kamuoyu* vs Lippmann’s plural/capital-letter distinction; *kanaat* = contentment/opinion; *rıza* = consent) so an English reader learns the same thing.
- **Titles and idioms**: “Kâsedeki kabuklular ve ‘kamuoyu’” became “Crustaceans in a bowl, and ‘public opinion’”; “Hiç yapılmamış bir düello” became “A duel that never took place”; “Hafıza kancası” became “Memory hook”; “Lasswell’ın tramvayı” became “Lasswell’s streetcar” (American term as in his text).
- **Committee on Public Information**: the Turkish gloss “Halkı Bilgilendirme Komitesi” was dropped in English.
- **Concept slugs**: `pseudo-environment`, `stereotype`, `manufacture-of-consent`, `phantom-public`, `lippmann-dewey-debate`, `propagandist-as-technician`. Lesson slug: `propaganda-and-public-opinion`.
- **`timeline`**: labels translated (same event ids).
- **First-person notes** of the Turkish source (“açıp okuduk”, “bulamadık”) remain first person plural in English (“we opened and read”, “we did not find”).
- **“Bizim yorumumuz”** became “Our interpretation” / “Our reading” everywhere.

## Doubts
1. **“sözde çevre”** vs “pseudo-environment”: English keeps Lippmann’s original word; the Turkish “sahte çevre” appears only in the terminology note and one card.
2. **“teknisyen propagandacı”** → “the propagandist as technician”: chosen consistently; it is a shorthand of the lesson, not Lasswell’s term, and both versions say so.
3. **“kamuoyları / büyük harfli Kamuoyu”** → “public opinions / Public Opinion in capital letters”: in English Lippmann’s own distinction is already available, so the Turkish workaround is explained only in `deep-turkish-terms`.
4. **Question wording**: the mini question in `today-lab-consent` (true/false) keeps the same answer index in both languages.
