# Research notes: lesson `social-thought-roots` (Toplumsal Düşüncenin Kökleri)

Date of research: 2026-09-20. All bibliography entries in `content/bibliography/social-thought-roots.json` are `verified.status: ok`. Lesson text: 29 core cards, 9 deep cards, 12 quiz questions; the `turkey` section is included (see (d)). Lesson status in `course.json` was set to `ready` only temporarily for validation and reverted.

Method: primary texts were read wherever a free full text exists (Comte's *Cours* vol. 4 and Durkheim's *Suicide* on Project Gutenberg; Durkheim's *Rules* 7th ed. on archive.org; Simmel's 1903 essay and 1908 *Soziologie* chapter IX at socio.ch; Weber's 1910 report at zeno.org; Marx and Engels on marxists.org). Everything else rests on reference works (SEP, IEP, IESS entries on Encyclopedia.com, TDV and Türk Maarif encyclopedias) and on Crossref/OpenAlex abstracts.

## (a) Could NOT verify, therefore left out (or used only with attribution)

**Comte, Spencer, Marx**
- The frequently told story that Comte gave up “physique sociale” because Quetelet had taken the name: **not verified**. Comte's own footnote (read) does not mention Quetelet. Two pages that state it as fact (a Cairn chapter, an Encyclopédie Universalis page) could not be opened (blocked/paywalled); French Wikipedia (“Sociophysique”) calls it a frequently repeated anecdote. The lesson says exactly that and cites only Quetelet's 1835 title (Open Library).
- Comte's birth day (SEP: 20 January 1798; Open Library: 19 January) and Simmel's death day (26 vs 28 September 1918): only years are used everywhere.
- Spencer's and Marx's own works were not opened; Spencer rests on SEP + IEP, Marx (apart from the *German Ideology* passage, which was read) on SEP + Open Library. Death places, Marx's birthplace and Spencer's works list are omitted (`works: []` for Spencer).
- Comte's *Cours* volume 4 was read in a Gutenberg e-text; the English translations (Martineau) were not opened.

**Durkheim**
- *Division of Labour* and *Elementary Forms* were not read; mechanical/organic solidarity, moral density, anomie and the “cult of the individual” come from the IEP entry (Carls). The Halls, Solovay–Mueller and Spaulding–Simpson translations were not opened.
- *Suicide*: read in the French Gutenberg e-text (1897 ed.). Left out: the Swiss canton figures (87/293 and 83/456; the unit is not stated beside the table and I did not want to guess “per million”), any criticism of Durkheim's use of official statistics or of the ecological reading of his rates (no source read), and page numbers. The “below 100 per million” figure for three German Catholic regions is Durkheim's own.
- *Rules*: the 7th edition (1919) was read, not the 1895 first edition; wording may differ from the first edition. The 1895 date and Alcan as publisher come from Durkheim's own footnote in *Suicide* and from the IEP.
- Durkheim's death place and birth day are omitted (IEP gives April 1858, Épinal; November 1917).

**Weber**
- *The Protestant Ethic* and *Economy and Society* were not read. The iron-cage passage comes from SEP (Kim) and Baehr's abstract; the 1930 date of Parsons's translation was seen only in Open Library and is **left out**.
- The claim in a search summary that Roth and Wittich render *Herrschaft* as “domination” and Gerth and Mills as “authority” (from Rigby 1966; Crossref-verified but not opened) is **not used**. The deep card uses only the Henderson–Parsons footnote (read on the German History Intersections site) and the SEP entry.
- Founding year of the German Sociological Society: Lepsius and the IESS entry on Tönnies point to 1909; the IESS entry on Simmel says 1910. The lesson gives no founding year.
- Weber's 1910 report was read in the reprint at zeno.org (*Gesammelte Aufsätze zur Soziologie und Sozialpolitik*, 2nd ed. 1988, pp. 431–449), not in the *Verhandlungen* (1911); only the page “42” given by Lepsius is known for the printed report. The article by Darmon and Frade (2013, *Max Weber Studies* 13(2)) was located through Crossref but not read; not cited.

**Simmel**
- The 1903 essay was read in German only. The lesson gives its title in Turkish/English as our own rendering; Wolff's 1950 translation was not opened. “Geistesleben” = “mental life” is the established English title; I did not check alternatives.
- *Philosophie des Geldes*, the fashion essays and Simmel's Berlin career details beyond IESS are omitted. The year Park studied with Simmel (1899–1900) is left out: Encyclopedia.com places Park in Berlin after his 1899 Harvard degree without a clear date, a search summary said 1899–1900.

**Tönnies**
- The 1887 German first edition (publisher, place) was not verified; the bibliography uses the Loomis translation (1957), the Cambridge edition (2001, Crossref) and the Turkish translation (2019). *Kritik der öffentlichen Meinung* (Berlin: Springer, 1922) was not read; only Heberle's summary is used. The 1933 loss of emeritus status and the death year come from the Encyclopedia of World Biography entry on the same Encyclopedia.com page as the IESS entry.
- “Alman Sosyoloji Derneği başkanı 1909–1933”: IESS only (Heberle).

**Media links**
- Shils and Young (1953), Birnbaum (1955): **not read** (paywalled); their arguments are taken from McGuigan's 2016 retrospective in *The Sociological Review*. Lukes (1975), Phillips (1974), Connell (1997), Baehr (2001): **abstracts only** (Crossref/OpenAlex). Phillips's page range (340–354) comes from Semantic Scholar/PubMed (Crossref gives only the start page).
- Coronation: the month (June 1953) comes from McGuigan; no exact date, no audience figures.
- Left out because not verified or owned by other lessons: Dayan and Katz (*Media Events*), Carey's ritual view, Le Bon, Tarde, Ortega, Park's own writings (only “studied with Simmel” and the dissertation title *Masse und Publikum* appear in the person-source page, not in the lesson text), Kornhauser, Parsons's role as canoniser (only Baehr's remark that Parsons rendered the iron cage). The names Park, Burgess, Parsons and Tarde/Le Bon appear only as plain text or not at all, never as `[[…]]` links.

**Turkish material**
- Gökalp rests on two encyclopedia entries only (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi; Türk Maarif Ansiklopedisi). Authors of both pages were not identified (`anonymous: true`). A search summary claimed that Gökalp's 1915 chair was the “second independent sociology chair in Europe after Durkheim's”: **not used** (no source read). The two pages differ slightly in emphasis (TDV: Salonica lectures; TMA: 1913 professorship, 1915 institute).
- Turkish terms attested in sources I opened: cemaat–cemiyet / topluluk–toplum (TÜBİTAK entry, Bulut), ideal tip (same), sosyal olgu, mekanik→organik toplum, hars ve medeniyet, içtimaiyat / ilm-i içtimâ (TDV, TMA). **Not verified in any Turkish source** and therefore marked `chosen`/`check` in the terminology file: anomi, anlama (Verstehen), rasyonelleşme, demir kafes / çelik kabuk, toplumsal olgu, toplumsallaşma biçimleri, mesafeli tutum, blazé tutum, modernlik, meşru egemenlik (otorite).
- The KÜRE Ansiklopedi page on “cemaat ve cemiyet” carries the same text as the TÜBİTAK entry and counts as one source. No Turkish translations of Durkheim, Weber or Simmel are mentioned (none verified).

## (b) Uncertainties and judgement calls

- **Encyclopedia.com / IESS.** The IESS entries (Parsons on Durkheim, Bendix on Weber, Mayntz on Simmel, Heberle on Tönnies) were read on Encyclopedia.com pages that also carry a second, unsigned entry; the year 1968 is taken from the IESS citation printed on the Park page (Hughes 1968). The IEP and SEP page authors are as displayed on the pages; IEP pages show no date.
- **Interpretations flagged as ours** in the text: the “shared question” summary of the lesson; Weber's 1910 plan as an early content analysis; Tönnies's public-opinion argument as resembling the spiral of silence (no influence claimed); the online reading of Simmel's stranger; the feed exercise; “yanlılığın seferber edilmesi” as our translation of *mobilization of bias*; “egemenlik (otorite)” for *Herrschaft*; the reading of media measurements as social facts; the remark that a condensed Martineau translation means English readers met a selected Comte.
- **Hypothetical scenarios**, all labelled (“Hayal et” / “Imagine”): the guild apprentice and his descendant, the two solidarity places, the auction hand-raising, the three authority scenes, the bureaucratic newsroom exercise, the community with intact bonds but no limits (anomie question), the student who calls a village pure Gemeinschaft, the public broadcaster on a day of mourning, Arda in the city.
- **Sensitive content.** The suicide typology and the Phillips card are academic; no methods are described, and both cards carry a note that the reader may skip them.
- **Numbers used**, all from sources read: 25,000 / 5,000 / 9,000 / 10,000 marks (Lepsius); 31 books, 256 articles, about 100 translations (IESS, citing Gassen and Landmann); Phillips 1947–1968; “below 100 per million” (Durkheim); 132 years (publisher's blurb via the bookseller listing).
- **`introducedBy`**: left empty for anomie, solidarity, verstehen and modernity because priority was not checked; set for sociology (Comte, verified), social-facts, ideal-type, rationalization, gemeinschaft-gesellschaft, metropolis-mental-life.
- **`minutes` in course.json (35)**: with 29 core cards the lesson will probably take closer to 40–45 minutes; left for the lead to decide.
- **Card length**: a few core cards are shorter than 150 words (`claim-legacy` 119, `people-press` 145, `con-anomie` 142, `how-suicide` 142); I preferred short and verified to longer and uncertain.
- The exam card lists alternative Turkish names only where a Turkish source attests them; it names no institution.
