## (a) Could NOT verify, therefore left out (or used only with attribution)

**Sources not opened in full (abstract or secondary description only)**
- Most journal articles (Squire 1988, Rugg 1941, Prior 2009, Scharkow 2016, Scharkow & Bachl 2017, OSC 2015, John et al. 2012, Matthes et al. 2015, Nosek et al. 2018, Rosenthal 1979, Glass 1976, Lombard et al. 2002, Hayes & Krippendorff 2007, Merton & Kendall 1946, Henrich et al. 2010, Sears 1986, Funder & Ozer 2019, Wasserstein & Lazar 2016, Slater 2007, Huesmann et al. 2003): Crossref record plus abstract (OpenAlex or publisher/ERIC page). The lesson uses only what the abstract states. Read in full: Lohr & Brick 2017, Simmons et al. 2011, Gerber et al. 2009, Parry et al. 2021, Lee 2010, Hopf et al. 2008, Martino 2023, Kramer et al. 2014 (PMC), the PNAS expression of concern, Bradburn 2000, Lincoln & Guba 1988, Peterson & Thurstone 1933 / Shuttleworth & May 1933 / Blumer 1933 (Internet Archive full-text scans), Livingstone & Das 2013, Morley 1992 (relevant chapter), Metin & Ünal 2022 and Ercan & Kan 2004 (abstracts and first pages).
- **Berelson 1952**: the book itself was not opened. His definition is quoted (with p. 18) in the Illinois open textbook "Reading Social Science Methods" and appears in method-handbook summaries; the lesson paraphrases it and does not quote it.
- **Rugg 1941**: paywalled. The 21% vs 39% figures come from Bradburn (2000, a conference paper), the 75% vs 54% figures from Frankovic (CBS, 2007). They are consistent (75/54 are the shares among respondents with an opinion), and the lesson says so.
- **Lincoln & Guba 1985**: the book was not opened; the four criteria and their pairing with internal validity, external validity, reliability and objectivity are taken from their own 1988 conference paper (ERIC), which cites the 1985 book at p. 301. Turkish renderings of the four criteria are ours (English terms given in brackets).
- **Cohen 1960 (kappa), Cronbach 1951, Cronbach & Meehl 1955, Campbell & Fiske 1959, Campbell & Stanley 1963, Shadish, Cook & Campbell 2002, Krippendorff's own book, Cohen 1988**: not used. The classic typology of validity (statistical conclusion, internal, construct, external) is therefore not presented; validity and reliability are defined in plain words and cited to Turkish and communication-methods sources that were opened.

**Facts deliberately omitted because sources disagree or I could not check them**
- **Erie County sample design.** Martino (2023) says the 600 panelists were chosen from an initial group of two thousand; a search summary of the book text says four groups of about 600 from 3,000, with three groups re-interviewed only once and the fourth interviewed monthly. Neither could be checked against the book (borrow-only). The lesson gives only what the Columbia University Press page and Martino agree on: 600 people, Erie County, Ohio, monthly interviews, May to November 1940 (the press page says "seven months", Martino "six months"; the lesson says "from May to November"). The control-group structure is not mentioned.
- **First publication of *The People's Choice*:** the Columbia UP page says "originally published in 1948"; Open Library and Sills 1987 (via the existing bibliography entry) say 1944. The lesson says the book "appeared in 1944".
- **Literary Digest forecast:** many secondary sources give Landon 57 percent; Lohr & Brick (2017, full text) give 54 percent and 370 electoral votes. The lesson uses 54. Gallup's own sample size (often given as 50,000) is not stated because I did not open a source that gives it.
- **Payne Fund publication years:** the three volumes read are dated 1933 (Macmillan, "Published November, 1933"), but some volumes of the twelve-study series (for example Edgar Dale's) are commonly dated later (1935). The lesson therefore says "the first volumes appeared in 1933". Jowett, Jarvie & Fuller (1996) was not opened and is not cited.
- **Eron et al. 1972 / Lefkowitz et al. 1972 / 1977 (Columbia County study):** abstracts were not available. A conference paper (Gentile, Linder & Walsh 2003) reports r = .31 for third-grade TV violence predicting aggression at 19 and r = .01 for the reverse, boys only; I could not find a second source, so **these numbers are left out**. The lesson cites only Huesmann et al. 2003 (abstract), Hopf et al. 2008 (which states the effect and selection hypotheses) and Slater 2007.
- **Ien Ang:** the lesson says only that she read forty-two viewers' letters written in reply to her own advertisement (Livingstone & Das 2013). That the ad ran in the Dutch women's magazine *Viva*, that the Dutch original (*Het geval Dallas*) is from 1982, and Ang's own caveat about representativeness come from a Dutch Wikipedia article and search summaries only, so they were **left out**. The place of publication of the English edition differs between sources (New York vs London) and is omitted from the entry.
- **Morley:** *Nationwide* is called a "television programme"; the sources I opened do not describe its format further. Morley's own description of the twenty-nine groups is used (group interviews chosen over individual ones; groups recruited in colleges or public places; limitation: not in the home).
- **Simmons et al.:** only the 61 percent figure for combining four flexibilities and the "nearly doubles" statement for two dependent variables are used. The single-flexibility percentages in Table 1 were garbled in my text extraction and are not used.
- **Kramer et al. 2014:** the affiliations of the authors are not stated (only "an experiment on Facebook"). The lesson says the expression of concern appeared "about a month" after the article (first published 2 June 2014; notice published online 3 July 2014).
- **Turkish terms:** only "güvenirlik/geçerlik" (Ercan & Kan 2004) and "içerik analizi" and "nitel araştırma" (Metin & Ünal 2022) were seen in Turkish sources I opened. "Güvenilirlik/geçerlilik" and "kodlayıcılar arası güvenirlik" were seen in titles or summaries of search results only. Every other term is `chosen` or `check` in `content/terminology/research-methods.json`.
- **Not used at all:** Hovland's Yale experiments (planned lesson `persuasion-yale`); Cantril's *Invasion from Mars* (already in `magic-bullet`); individual fraud cases in psychology and political science (named individuals; the lesson relies on aggregate evidence instead); Many Labs and Camerer et al. replication projects (not checked); registered reports (not checked); Twitter-based prediction critiques (Gayo-Avello 2011: abstract only); Krippendorff's unit definitions (sampling, recording, context units); the four-part typology of validity; panel attrition and panel conditioning (mentioned in one sentence as general methods knowledge, labelled "our addition").

## (b) Uncertainties and judgment calls

- **Definitions of standard concepts** (probability vs convenience sampling, random assignment, internal and external validity, confounding, cross-sectional vs panel, ethnography, in-depth interview) are our own plain-language explanations of textbook concepts. They are not attributed to a page-level source; where a source exists it is cited (Ercan & Kan for reliability and validity; Lincoln & Guba for the qualitative counterparts).
- **Our interpretations, labelled in the text:** Morley's group interviews resemble the focus-group logic; the split-sample "imaginary" panel example (10 percent moving each way); the "reinforcing spiral" illustration; the reading that "method may be part of the result" in the limited-effects debate; that combining flawed studies combines the flaw; Ang's letter writers are self-selected; the Turkish "content analysis" naming muddle is "a matter of names".
- **Hypothetical scenarios (all labelled "Hayal et"/"Imagine" or "our example"):** the 200,000-user pop-up, the tweet collection, the four questions of the method-choice lab, the student-newspaper video study, the 5-million-user notification experiment, the 24-person attention-span study, the campus-app survey, the "70 percent" headline, the 12-person interview study, the erroneous-measurement survey, and the two-coder skew example (the arithmetic 0.95² + 0.05² is ours).
- **"Payne Fund: three designs":** only three of the twelve studies are described, chosen because their methods differ. The full-text scans were read for Peterson & Thurstone (Preface, Introduction, first experiment, start of the persistence chapter), Shuttleworth & May (Chapter I) and Blumer (Procedure). Blumer's chapter on sex conduct (which he says he omitted) is not mentioned.
- **Quotations:** no direct quotations longer than a few words; "a matter of concern" (PNAS notice, English version) is the only quoted phrase and is attributed.
- **Card length:** all cards are within the 150–300 word range in the English version; the Turkish versions are shorter by word count (agglutinative language): the shortest core cards (`how-design`, 146 words) and one deep card (`deep-focus`, 139) are slightly under 150.
- **`minutes` in course.json (35):** with 24 core cards this lesson will probably take 35–45 minutes; left for the lead to decide.
- **Concept `introducedBy`:** empty for all new concepts (Glass, Berelson etc. have no person entries and were not created).
