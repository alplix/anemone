# Translation notes: `propaganda-history` (English, 2026-09-20)

Files: `content/i18n/en/courses/mass-communication-theories/lessons/propaganda-history.json`, people `creel bernays ellul`, concepts `propaganda committee-on-public-information propaganda-white-grey-black atrocity-propaganda public-relations agitprop`. Status `machine` on all.

## Adaptations
- **Spelling**: British (colour, organised), matching the existing English files; “grey” (British) is used for the middle colour and “gray” only inside quoted source names.
- **Structure**: card ids, sections, layers, kinds, question ids, question types, options, answer indexes, match pairs, order items, cloze blanks and every `[[person:…]]`, `[[concept:…]]`, `[[lesson:…]]`, `[[cite:…]]` are identical to the Turkish source (checked by a script before writing, and by the validator). The correct answers of many multiple-choice questions were moved to varied positions in both languages by the same permutation; the ordinal references in the explanations (“the first option…”) were remapped by the same script, so the explanations still point at the right option.
- **Turkish terms stay in Turkish where the lesson is about the Turkish word.** The deep card `deep-turkish-terms` (propaganda / yaymaca, telkin, yaygara, ikna, ajitasyon, kara/beyaz/gri propaganda) keeps the Turkish words in italics with English glosses, because the card is about how those words are used in Turkish. “Halkla ilişkiler”, “Kamuyu Bilgilendirme Komitesi” and “Halkı Aydınlatma ve Propaganda Bakanlığı” are given in Turkish once in the exam card with the note that the renderings are the lesson’s own. Turkish letters therefore occur in the English file in exactly those places (İkna, Süleyman, ilişkiler, Aydınlatma and similar).
- **Quotations**: the Turkish text paraphrases the English sources; the English version gives the same short quotations in their original English wording (“the world’s greatest adventure in advertising”, “invisible government”, “fine old word”, “a plain lie”, “stroke of genius”, “mustard seed of truth”, “no authority to administer an oath”, “deliberate and systematically organised massacres”, “substantially accurate”, “emotional blackmail”), each under 15 words, without changing what the Turkish says.
- **Turkey section**: the Ankara inscription is kept, with “Ancyra” (the Roman name of Ankara) explained inside the first sentence; nothing else in the story needs adapting for a foreign reader. The Turkish word “Amasya” is kept as “Amasya” (Amasia in the Loeb text).
- **Humour and voice**: the informal “sen” address became a direct “you”; the dry asides (“a fine old word”, “the hardest thing about war propaganda is this”) were kept in spirit. A pun that only works in Turkish: none.
- **Numbers**: Turkish thousands separator (6.500, 65.000) became the English comma (6,500, 65,000); years, article titles and the ministry’s German name are unchanged.

## Doubts
- “Uncle by marriage” for the Turkish “enişte” in the Bernays biography is a functional rendering (the husband of one’s aunt).
- The English term for TDK’s *yaymaca* is left untranslated; it is a Turkish coinage that the lesson only glosses.
- The Turkish “kadavra fabrikası” is rendered “corpse factory”, the story’s common English name; the German original (Kadaververwertungsanstalt) and the 1917/1925 debate about “Kadaver” are kept in both languages.
- The description strings were shortened to 160 characters or fewer in both languages.
