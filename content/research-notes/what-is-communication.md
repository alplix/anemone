## (a) Could not verify, therefore left out (or explicitly hedged)

Definitions and counts
- **“Dance (1970) examined 95 definitions.”** Only one page I opened (an open organizational-communication textbook on LibreTexts) and search snippets say 95; the OUP abstract page and Littlejohn & Foss 2008 give “15 main themes” and the three critical splits but no count. The lesson uses only what two sources confirm: 15 themes and three splits (Dance 1970), 126 definitions (Dance & Larson 1976, via Littlejohn & Foss 2008 note 3 and Nastasia & Rakow 2005).
- **The list of Dance's 15 components** (symbols, understanding, interaction, …) appears in search-result summaries only; not enumerated in the lesson.
- **Wright (1959) and Janowitz (1968) definitions of mass communication.** Widely quoted, but I could not open either text (the Wright book is lending-only; no reference work opened quoted Janowitz). Left out; Maletzke (1963) is used instead, confirmed by the German Wikipedia article and Ioni & Pstae 2017.
- **Lasswell's three functions and Wright's fourth (entertainment).** Only one reference-work page (iResearchNet, Functionalism) and search-result summaries; the SAGE encyclopedia entry redirects to a login; Wikipedia's Mass communication article does not mention them. Left out on purpose; they belong to the planned lesson `functions-of-media`. The lesson therefore covers “functions” through Carey's two purposes (transmission/ritual) and Watzlawick's content/relationship aspects, not through Lasswell.
- **Berelson & Steiner (1964) “transmission of information, ideas, emotions, skills, by symbols”.** Two secondary sources quote it but disagree on the page (p. 254 in Littlejohn & Foss note 11, p. 527 in Nastasia & Rakow). Not used as a quotation; only a generic “transmission of information” definition is described in the deep card on Dance (attributed to “a definition”, not to Berelson & Steiner).
- **Hovland, Janis & Kelley (1953), Hall (1963) proxemics**: not verified, not used.

Watzlawick and colleagues
- **Exact wording of the five axioms.** I could not open the book (Internet Archive lending copy returned 401/“not available”) and the MIT course-reader copy of chapter 2 sits behind an Anubis bot-check page (not bypassed). The wording and page numbers used (pp. 51, 54, 59, 66-67, 70) come from Lutterer 2007, who quotes them with pages. Other secondary accounts word axioms 3-5 differently (e.g. “dependent on the punctuation of the partners' communication procedures”, “inter-human communication procedures are either symmetric or complementary”). The lesson therefore **paraphrases** the axioms and quotes only the four-word first one.
- **Bavelas (1990) reply to Motley**: existence, authorship, journal and pages confirmed via Crossref; its content was not opened (publisher page 403). A search-engine summary says she proposed treating “all behavior is communication” and “one cannot not communicate” as separate hypotheses rather than axioms; this is **not** used in the lesson (single, unopened source). Andersen (1991) and Motley (1991) are known only by their Crossref records and by Littlejohn & Foss's note 13.
- **Birth year of Watzlawick**: family-authorised obituary implies 1921/22 (aged 85 on 31 March 2007); Encyclopedia.com, Deutsche Biographie (NDB) and Wikipedia say 25 July 1921; a local memorial page says 1922 and 24 March 2007. 1921 and 31 March 2007 are used; discrepancy noted.
- **Carey's birth year**: not entered in the person file (Wikipedia gives 1934, obituaries give “age 71” in May 2006, which only implies 1934/35). Death (2006) is confirmed by two obituaries.
