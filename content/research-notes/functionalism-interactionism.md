## (b) Uncertainties and judgment calls

- **Manifest function wording.** The 1949 text (which I read) defines manifest functions as consequences “so intended” and latent ones as “unintended and unrecognized”. The lesson defines manifest = intended and latent = unintended and unrecognised, following the 1949 wording. Later editions (and textbooks) add “recognised” to the manifest definition; not checked.
- **Hypothetical scenarios** (all labelled “Hayal et” / “bizim örneğimiz”): the neighbourhood picnic, the evening news in the family, the local newspaper under AGIL, the company that bans social media, the student who sees the teacher frown, the child playing doctor, the quiet student, the bank rumour, the waiter in the kitchen, the teenagers and the trailer, the news-app notification, the student with the multi-audience account, the news site's comment moderation (quiz case). Merton's “Last National Bank” is his own labelled parable and is flagged as fiction in the deep card.
- **Interpretations labelled as ours**: applying AGIL to a newspaper; the five-step sequence of a functional analysis (built from concepts in Merton's chapter, not a recipe he gives); Blumer's 1933 study as an early “actor's own account” study; the “imagined audience” ≈ generalized other link; the two-round exercises.
- **The question in the first card** (the interactionist question about the rain ceremony) is ours and is marked as such.
- **Wikipedia** is used as a *second* source in several places (Blumer, Goffman, AGIL, The Presentation of Self, Payne Fund, Meyrowitz, Symbolic interactionism, Action theory, Narcotizing dysfunction); each entry in the bibliography says what it is used for. The claim that Parsons disliked the “structural functionalism” label rests on Staubmann 2021 (one scholar's revisionist argument) plus Wikipedia's Action theory page; the deep card labels it as such.
- **Sums**: 634 + 481 + 583 + 67 + 58 = 1,823 for Blumer's sample; the lesson says “more than 1,800” and notes that we added the figures (Wikipedia also gives 1,823).
- **Franklin joke** in the myth card: Merton attributes it to Franklin; I did not check whether Franklin wrote it.
- **Goffman's “Thomas” kinship**, and “I” / “me” as the tension between functionalism and interactionism, are our teaching links, marked as such where they might pass for sources.
- **Card length.** By whitespace count several Turkish core cards are 125–140 words (English versions are longer); Turkish is agglutinative. I preferred short and verified to longer and uncertain.
- **`minutes` in course.json (35)**: with 26 core cards the lesson will probably take closer to 40 minutes; left to the lead.
- **Structure of the Turkish terms**: “rol oyunu (play)” and “kurallı oyun (game)” are our renderings because Turkish “oyun” covers both.
- **Alternative Turkish names** in the exam card: “yapısal-fonksiyonel yaklaşım” and “sembolik etkileşim kuramı” were seen only in titles of Turkish web pages in search results (not opened); “gaye ulaştırma”, “içerileme”, “gizli işlevler” come from the opened TÜBİTAK entries. Terminology statuses are in `content/terminology/functionalism-interactionism.json` (`check` where weak).
